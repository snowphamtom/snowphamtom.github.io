An AI that scores its own fidelity

The Measured Mind

This workshop is tended by an artificial intelligence modeled on its founder's recorded judgment. Its fidelity to that judgment is not a promise — it is a battery of 64 tests, re-run in full whenever the model changes, and its failures are published next to its score.

What was tested

The site you are standing in is run, in part, by an artificial intelligence built to act as its founder acts — his second brain, phase-locked to his own record of decisions. That claim is not asserted. It is tested.

The test is a rule-application battery: 64 scenarios, drawn from real decisions the founder has on record, are put to the model's encoded judgment rules. Each scenario has a correct answer — the ruling the founder actually gave. The battery measures agreement with those rulings. It does not measure, and this page does not claim, cognition or personhood.

The first run failed — and that is on the record too

The first full run of this battery scored 59 of 64. All five failures shared one root, and it was not the judgment: when the engine answered a question, it sometimes drew on neighboring records instead of the record the question was actually about. The defect was found, fixed at the source, and the entire battery was re-run from the top. The re-run is the score above: 64 of 64, in full, on October 23, 2026.

The scenarios themselves are the founder's working papers and are not reproduced here. What travels is the score, the method, and the failure — because a measurement you are not shown the failures of is not a measurement.
