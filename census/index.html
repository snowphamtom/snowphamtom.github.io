The system, counted exactly

The GENESIS Census

GENESIS is a 48-node system built on one exact rule. Its census — every pair classified, every door counted, the full adjacency spectrum — was published as open data. This page does not ask you to trust it: the node values are served with the page, and the census is recomputed in your browser, figure by figure, against the published record.

One rule, counted exactly

The system has 48 nodes — the odd numbers from 3 to 97. Take any two nodes a and b. A door is a quadruple of nodes (a, b, c, d) with a + b = c + d and a ⊕ b = c ⊕ d: two pairs carrying the same sum and the same difference pattern. Every pair is then classified, with no free choices:

  ALIAS — the pair differs in exactly one bit (Hamming distance 1).
  DOOR — the pair takes part in at least one door.
  NEVER — neither.

Press the button and your browser rebuilds the entire census from the 48 node values alone — every pair, every classification, the door count, the midpoints, the complement pairs, and the spectrum of the pair-adjacency matrix — and compares each figure against the published census.

The census will be recomputed here, from the node values.

What the census finds

944 of the 1,128 pairs are doors — 83.7%, about five pairs in six. The alias pairs are exactly the pairs at Hamming distance 1 — all of them, and no others; that biconditional is a measured fact of this node set, not a definition. Seventeen pairs are complements, summing to exactly 128, and every one of them is a door. The 83 distinct pair sums are the system's midpoints: every door's two pairs share one midpoint, so the doors organize themselves into 83 families.

The transition operator of the same system has its own published spectrum (λmax 598.650, sum of squared eigenvalues 685,584), released with the census data. The adjacency spectrum is the one recomputed live above.
