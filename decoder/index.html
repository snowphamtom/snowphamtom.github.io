The Decoder Bench

The refusal discipline that runs through this house's work, built as hardware: two decoders that repair exactly what they can prove and refuse everything else. Their safety property is not argued — it is enumerated, all 143,005 fault patterns of it, in your browser.

Repair, or refuse

Each decoder guards a 48-column code. A received word is summed, column by column, into a 14-component syndrome. The syndrome decides everything:

  CLEAN — the syndrome is zero. Nothing arrived damaged.
  REPAIR — the syndrome is exactly one signed column: the damage is a single column, named and corrected.
  TAIL — the syndrome is exactly one axis step: a tail edit, named.
  REFUSE — anything else. The decoder will not guess.

The property that makes this safe is exact: no combination of two or three damaged columns may produce a syndrome that looks clean, repairable, or like a tail edit. If it held, every double and triple fault is refused, never silently miscorrected. Below, your browser tests it exhaustively — all 143,005 fault patterns per decoder (every single, double, and triple column fault, with signs, plus the zero pattern and the 28 tail edits) — and tallies the verdicts against the golden model.
